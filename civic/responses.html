Why engagement deserves recognition

Public service involves more than holding a position. When an official takes time to acknowledge a resident's concerns, explain a decision, or provide records, that action creates an opportunity for informed public dialogue.

This project recognizes documented engagement without assuming that every response signals agreement. An acknowledgment is not an endorsement. A substantive reply is not necessarily a commitment to act. And the absence of a recorded reply does not establish an official's motives, beliefs, or conduct.

Our goal is to recognize constructive participation while keeping the evidence available for residents to judge for themselves.

How we count and verify

Editorial standard: Every published entry should have a verified date, a faithful summary, and a source that may be cited publicly. Private emails should be quoted or linked only with appropriate permission or a lawful public-record basis. Personal contact information should be removed.

Acknowledgment: Confirms receipt or thanks the sender, without a substantive policy position.

Substantive reply: Addresses the report's questions, facts, policy, or next steps.

Documented action: A verifiable vote, resolution, public statement, hearing request, records disclosure, or other official step. One communication may be categorized only once in the headline counts; additional actions are tracked separately.

Not recorded: Means no verified entry has been published here. It does not mean silence, support, opposition, or neglect.
